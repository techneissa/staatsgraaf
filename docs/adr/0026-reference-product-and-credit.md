# ADR 0026: The reference product, what we take from it, and the credit

Date: 05-10-2026
Status: accepted. This ADR is an engineering decision, not legal advice. The owner or a lawyer should confirm the points under "To confirm".

## Context

Staatsgraaf was inspired by the Gov Graph at https://graph.civlab.org/us, a public map of the United States federal government made by CivLab (`docs/02-reference-system-spec.md`). The captured CivLab pages state no licence, so default copyright applies to everything on them (`research/O-licenses.md`).

A review on 05-10-2026 compared the tree that was about to become public with the captures of the reference. Shipped code and data are independent: no markup, class string, stylesheet rule, SVG path, long text or data record of the reference is in `web/`, `shared/` or `data/`. The exposure was in documents. Two research notes and the reference spec transcribed the reference's expression (class strings, colour tables, layout constants, whole data records, named foreign officials with image addresses), and some wording said "copy" and "match the look".

## Decision

1. **What we take: ideas and information architecture.** A persistent canvas beside an editorial column, a graph of bodies, positions, people and typed relations, a change feed, news with inline entity links, an overview, topic and budget pages, search, a legend. These are functions and layouts, and `docs/02` describes them in our own words.
2. **What we do not take: expression.** No code, markup, CSS, class names, design tokens by name or value, icons or glyph paths, text, data records or images from the reference. This holds for the shipped product and for the documents in the public repository.
3. **Documents describe behaviour, not the reference's expression.** `research/M-design.md` and `research/N-reference-model.md` are rewritten to observed behaviour and our own decisions. Field names of the reference's data may be listed as an observed vocabulary; records, people and image addresses may not. Examples are invented.
4. **Clean-room rule for new work.** Write behaviour in prose. Keep captures of the reference out of the repository and out of a builder's context. Never paste its class names, hex values or SVG. A second person checks any new screen against this rule. Where our UI needs a label the reference also has, we write our own.
5. **Wording.** The goal is to learn from the reference's information architecture. It is not to match its look. Older records keep their date and get a dated note instead of a rewrite.
6. **Product overlaps nudged.** One exact sentence, three home headings and the light-theme neutral ramp were changed so that nothing in the shipped product is a near copy.
7. **The credit.** Staatsgraaf names CivLab as the inspiration, thanks them for the idea, and states that it is independent: not affiliated with or endorsed by CivLab, and using none of its code, data, text or images. The text is in `README.md` (section Inspiration), in `ATTRIBUTION.md` (generated by `pipelines/licenses.mjs`) and on the method page, in Dutch and English. Plain text links only. No logo, no wordmark, no use of "Gov Graph" outside the credit. The credit is true only while decisions 1 to 3 hold; a change that brings reference material into the repository needs this ADR revisited first.
8. **A courtesy message.** The owner sends CivLab a short note before or at publication, so they can say if the credit reads wrong or something looks too close. A reply is not legal clearance. Nothing was sent by an agent.

## What stays a shared vocabulary

Nine relation type names (`elects`, `appoints`, `oversees`, `advises`, `office`, `dept_head`, `confirms`, `ex_officio`, `administers`) and a few field names appear in both data models. They are short functional identifiers, our schema adds its own, and renaming them across the data, schema, pipelines and web would cost much for little gain. They stay (`research/N-reference-model.md`).

## To confirm (owner or lawyer)

1. Whether the "inspired by" credit plus the courtesy message is enough, or written consent is wanted before publishing.
2. Whether CivLab has terms not visible on the captured pages.
3. The weight of the shared home-page order (news, people, changes, overview, budget) in a dispute about overall look and feel.

## Consequences

- Readers and builders get clear rules, and the public tree holds nothing of CivLab's that would need a licence.
- `docs/02` and the research notes are less detailed about the reference. That is intended: they hold what we rely on, not a transcription.
- Rewording older records is limited to the specific phrases. History stays readable through the dated notes.
